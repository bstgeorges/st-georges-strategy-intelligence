Executive readout

A change is not safe because it is technically complete

Mistral's legacy-code work, new third-party resilience standards, exploited vulnerabilities and misconduct intelligence all lead to the same test: can the firm show the baseline, owner, intervention authority and evidence before a change becomes business as usual?

Lead signal13 Sept 2026

Can the firm prove a change is safe before it spreads?

The hard part of AI-assisted change is not generating a result. It is retaining a usable baseline, deciding who can approve the next step, and showing how the result was tested, challenged or reversed.

  • Choose one live change and record the behaviour that must not move without approval.
  • Name the person who can pause, accept an exception or require rollback.
  • Put the evidence pack in the next committee meeting before the change reaches wider use.

Committee challenge

Questions that should have an answer this week

The aim is a short, inspectable answer: a named owner, an evidence location and the next decision.

  1. Which current change would cause the most harm if its output, data route or provider behaviour changed tomorrow—and what is the approved baseline?
  2. Who can pause that workflow, accept an exception or require rollback, and how would they know that intervention is needed?
  3. Can the next committee pack show the test result, dependency map, exception record and the evidence that the agreed control actually operated?
