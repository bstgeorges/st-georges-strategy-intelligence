Executive readout

When agents interact, resilience becomes a system test

BIS Project Agenti is testing a controlled payment simulator to examine how agents may affect timing, liquidity demand, gridlock and shock propagation. Japan’s finance minister says G7 leaders agreed to AI-risk tabletop exercises; their design and dates are unspecified.

Lead signal14 Oct 2026

Test interaction, not each agent alone.

Controls that work in isolation may still produce correlated delays or disruption to shared services when agents respond to the same signal. BIS is exploring this in controlled research, not reporting a live failure or setting a supervisory requirement.

  • Simulate a common liquidity shock alongside loss of a shared service.
  • Measure payment delays, containment and minimum-service recovery.
  • Assign the first missing recovery step to the payments or resilience owner.

Committee challenge

Questions that should have an answer this week

The aim is a short, inspectable answer: a named owner, an evidence location and the next decision.

  1. If several agents respond to the same liquidity signal, what evidence shows their combined behaviour will not create avoidable payment delays or gridlock?
  2. Which critical payment or orchestration service would stop several agents at once, and can minimum service be restored if its normal control plane is unavailable?
  3. What independent evidence supports the provider’s claims about agent performance, limitations and safeguards in the workflow we plan to use?
