Operating readout

Change only becomes safe when its evidence travels with it

This week is about a familiar failure: a technical, supplier or regulatory change moves into live use while the evidence for its safety, ownership and recovery remains scattered.

What changed
Mistral describes an AI-assisted legacy-code migration built around parity testing and human review. BIS has opened a consultation on FMI cyber resilience and third-party reliance; ASIC, CISA and CNIL each show a different intake-to-action control problem.
Our judgement
External change is a control event. The change record needs to join the affected service, data, dependency, decision-maker, test result and recovery route while the work is still underway.
Why it matters
Speed creates a blind spot when the person approving a change cannot see the full dependency or when incident, complaint and vulnerability signals never reach the person who can act.
Committee question
Before one live change moves further, can management show who owns it, what it touches, what proves it works and how it can be stopped or recovered?
What to ask for
One current change record: the affected-service and data map, named authority, parity or control test, supplier position, approved exception, recovery route and evidence of closure.
Evidence and sources
Mistral's legacy-modernisation account, the CPMI-IOSCO publication, ASIC misconduct data, CISA's KEV update, AWS's CloudTrail guide and CNIL's e-invoicing guidance.

Control lessons

Failure patterns to test internally

These cards turn public events into usable internal challenge: what happened, what control lesson follows, and what question a firm should ask before the next committee pack.

AI-assisted change

A parity harness is a control, not a technical nice-to-have

What happened
Mistral describes using a numerical parity harness and human review while agents modernised a complex legacy system.
Control lesson
Agent-assisted change needs a known baseline, testable outputs, a review gate and a record of the decision to move work into production.

Question Can we show that one AI-assisted production change still behaves as intended, and who can stop it if it does not?

Source / Mistral, 9 Sept

Active exploitation

Exploited vulnerabilities test the exception process, not only the patch SLA

What happened
CISA added three actively exploited vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities Catalog on 11 September.
Control lesson
Firms need fast asset identification, a recorded remediation decision, compensating controls where patching is delayed, and explicit expiry for every exception.

Question Can we show every affected asset, the decision on each one, and evidence that the residual risk was accepted or removed?

Source / CISA, 11 Sept

Control intelligence

Misconduct reporting needs an owned route into action

What happened
ASIC says retail-investor issues and governance matters made up more than four in five misconduct reports in the first half of 2026.
Control lesson
Complaints, whistleblowing and scam intelligence need triage, investigation, accountable action and feedback into the control environment.

Question Can we follow one customer or colleague report from intake to decision, action and evidenced outcome?

Source / ASIC, 10 Sept

Incident evidence

Containment and recovery should leave one coherent decision record

What happened
AWS's second CloudTrail investigation guide takes an incident through containment, recovery and post-incident analysis.
Control lesson
Incident teams need the activity record, authority for each decision, recovery evidence and proof that the service was safe to resume.

Question Could we reconstruct one incident decision from alert to safe recovery without relying on memory?

Source / AWS, 3 Sept

Data accountability

Platform roles need to match the data trail in practice

What happened
CNIL's live e-invoicing guidance explains the controller-processor boundary, retention, contractual and traceability expectations around approved platforms.
Control lesson
A platform's stated role, actual data use, access controls, retention and audit trail need to agree before the implementation becomes hard to unwind.

Question Can we show the actual controller-processor position and data trail for one platform before a customer or regulator asks?

Source / CNIL, 10 Sept

Executive challenge

Three questions from the week

This is the most portable part of the edition: it gives the reader something they can carry into a committee, 1:1, or control review.

  1. Before one current change moves further, can we show the affected service, data and supplier map, the decision-maker and the test that gives us confidence?
  2. Which critical dependency would leave us unable to contain, recover or explain a decision if it failed today—and when was that route tested?
  3. Can we turn one report, alert or data-control exception into an owned action, independent retest and closed-loop outcome before it becomes an external finding?

Control intelligence

Complaint and vulnerability data should be treated as operating signals, not queue volume

Customer reports, control exceptions and active exploits become valuable only when they reach a person who can investigate, choose an intervention and account for the result.

Why now: ASIC and CISA show the same weak point from different directions: the signal arrives, but the ownership and closure record are not always connected.

Audience: Financial crime, cyber, conduct, customer operations, risk and internal audit.
