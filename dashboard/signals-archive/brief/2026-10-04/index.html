Why it matters

Controls can fail between steps. If the receiving person or system cannot see the authorised instruction, the comparison and any exception, they cannot challenge or own the final record.

What to do

Replay one high-impact record from instruction to decision. Collect the evidence each step receives and ask the next owner to resolve a mismatch. Use the first gap you find to assign one specific control fix.

The weekly readout

A control is only as strong as its hand-off.

A control may work at its source and still fail when evidence, authority or responsibility passes to the next person or system.

01 / What happened

Evidence is moving between people, systems and custodians.

OpenAI reports that Chatham is comparing an AI-assisted transaction-validation application with experienced reviewers. The SEC has proposed new crypto-custody pathways; the NCA reports a post-conviction confiscation order; Ireland’s DPC has ordered DPIAs and controls after paper-record failings; and CISA has added two vulnerabilities to its known-exploited catalog.

02 / Why it matters

A record can be complete at one point and unusable at the next.

Reviewers need to know what was authorised, what changed, which exception was raised and who accepted the result. Custody and disposal controls matter just as much as the system that created the record.

03 / What to do

Replay one record from authority to final decision.

Choose a transfer that can change a customer, trading or compliance outcome. Follow the original instruction through the evidence received and the final decision.

04 / Evidence

Find who can reject or correct a mismatch.

Keep the source record, comparison, exception disposition and receiving owner together. The first missing link is the next control fix.

Reading the signals

Where the dependency sits in the hand-off

Three places to ask for the evidence that survives a transfer.

AI-assisted review

A faster comparison still needs a challenge path.

Chatham says its application compares transaction terms and flags discrepancies, while results are checked against experienced reviewers before broader automation. The useful evidence is how a mismatch reaches a qualified decision-maker.

Source / OpenAI case study, 2 Oct

Custody choices

The SEC proposal would add possible custody routes.

The proposal would allow self-custody in specified circumstances and state trust companies as custodians. It is not adopted; firms can use the consultation to assess access, recovery and oversight choices against their current arrangements.

Source / SEC, 1 Oct

Record stewardship

Physical records and disposal need named controls too.

The DPC’s summary describes paper health records and confidential waste at one facility, followed by orders for finalised DPIAs and implementation of measures. The full decision is pending; the reported facts still prompt a practical custody and disposal check.

Source / DPC Ireland, 1 Oct

Executive challenge

Three questions from the week

Carry one into a committee, 1:1 or control review.

  1. At which point does evidence change hands in a decision-critical process, and who can reject or correct it?
  2. If a fund or adviser changes a crypto-custody arrangement, who controls access, recovery and the records needed to prove what happened?
  3. Can we show who safeguards sensitive records through use, storage, transfer and disposal?
