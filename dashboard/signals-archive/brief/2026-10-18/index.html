When agents interact, resilience becomes a system test.

What happened

BIS’s Project Agenti is testing a controlled payment simulator to examine how agents may affect timing, liquidity demand, gridlock and shock propagation. Japan’s finance minister says G7 leaders agreed to AI-risk tabletop exercises; design and dates are unspecified.

Why it matters

Test interaction, not each agent alone. Controls that work in isolation may still produce correlated delays or disruption to shared services when agents respond to the same signal. BIS is exploring this, not reporting a live failure or setting a supervisory requirement. The Bank of England’s September FPC record links frontier-AI incidents with interconnected cyber and operational risks.

What to do

Ask payments and resilience owners to simulate a common liquidity shock alongside loss of a shared service. Measure payment delays, containment and minimum-service recovery.

The weekly readout

Can we contain and recover from the combined effects of interacting agents?

BIS is testing a controlled payment simulator in which agents represent payment-system participants. Japan’s finance minister says G7 leaders agreed to future AI-risk tabletop exercises; the statement does not specify their design or dates. These are research and policy signals, not a live failure or a new requirement.

01 / What happened

Central banks are modelling interaction, not just single agents.

BIS Project Agenti will explore how agents with different objectives and payment strategies could affect timing, liquidity use, gridlock and shock propagation in a controlled simulator. It is ongoing work, not a result showing that live wholesale payments have been disrupted.

02 / Why it matters

Controls can fail through correlation.

Several agents may respond to the same signal and shift payment timing or liquidity demand together. The Bank of England’s September Financial Policy Committee record links frontier-AI incidents with interconnected cyber and operational risks. A shared service failure can make the combined effect harder to contain.

03 / What to do

Simulate a common shock and a shared-service loss.

Ask payments and resilience owners to model several agents responding to one liquidity signal while a critical service fails. Measure payment delays, containment and minimum-service recovery.

04 / Evidence

Ask for evidence of the combined outcome.

Retain agent objectives, shared inputs, payment queues, liquidity usage, dependency failures, intervention points and recovery times. State which assumptions came from the simulator and which outcomes were observed in the firm’s own exercise.

  • BIS is testing payment simulations in which agents represent different system participants.
  • Several individually controlled agents may still react to a shared signal and shift payment timing or liquidity demand together.
  • Test a common liquidity shock alongside loss of a shared service, then measure containment and minimum-service recovery.
  • Assign the first missing recovery step to the payments or resilience owner.

Reading the signals

Different decisions require different evidence

Separate controlled research from observed outcomes, map shared dependencies, and test recovery when agents respond to the same shock.

Collective behaviour

Individually sound decisions can still interact badly.

BIS Project Agenti represents payment-system participants with agents that have different objectives and strategies. The project will explore system effects in an auditable simulator before any scaled deployment.

Source / BIS Project Agenti

Governance reference

The FSB’s final sound practices are a toolkit, not a new rule.

The 12 practices encourage boards and senior management to refine existing AI governance, risk management and control frameworks. Map them to current processes and record specific gaps.

Executive challenge

Three questions from the week

Carry one into a committee, 1:1 or control review.

  1. If several agents react to the same liquidity signal, what evidence shows their combined behaviour will not create avoidable delays or gridlock?
  2. Which critical payment or orchestration service would stop several agents at once, and can minimum service be restored if the normal control plane is unavailable?
  3. What independent evidence supports the provider’s claims about agent performance, limitations and safeguards in the workflow we plan to use?
