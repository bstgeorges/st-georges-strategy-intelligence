Week ending 15 Nov 2026 · Edition 22

A stop instruction is not the same as containment.

Archive record · The Weekly Judgement published with this edition is retained here in full.

15 November 2026: clarified how shared-provider concentration relates to the stop and recovery judgement.

What happened

OpenAI says its Control Room stops further model output within 35 seconds after receiving a stop instruction in its research infrastructure. Separately, APRA says some individual providers support institutions holding more than half of APRA-regulated financial assets.

Why it matters

The two observations describe different environments and neither proves a firm can control its own agents or recover its services. A stop timer begins after someone or something decides to intervene; it does not include detection, human decision-making or the reversal of actions already taken. Concentration matters here because a stop or recovery path that depends on the same provider may fail with it.

What to do

Choose one material agentic workflow and run a controlled stop exercise. Timestamp the alert, decision and independent enforcement; identify actions that remain queued or complete during the interval, then demonstrate the recovery or compensating action for one consequential outcome.

Reading the signals

Separate the decision to stop from the outcome

Each source answers a different operating question.

Provider account

Control Room bounds further model output.

OpenAI says Control Room can halt further model output within 35 seconds after receiving a stop instruction and denies inference if the control service is unavailable. This is its account of research infrastructure, not proof that completed actions can be reversed or that an enterprise deployment has the same control.

Source / OpenAI

Supervisory outlook

Shared providers can transmit disruption.

APRA says AI can create risks within firms and accelerate external threats, while shared material providers can transmit disruption. Some individual providers support institutions holding more than half of APRA-regulated financial assets; this is Australian supervisory evidence, not a measurement of any individual firm’s exposure.

Source / APRA

Threat research

Patch the application and investigate the host.

Microsoft describes attackers using an outbound relay after compromising internet-facing applications. Patching the initial flaw does not itself remove the relay client or persistence; the report describes observed telemetry rather than the prevalence of compromise across banks.

Source / Microsoft

Executive challenge

Three questions from the week

Ask for evidence of intervention, recovery and closure.

  1. How long does it take to stop a material agent after an intervention decision, and what can still happen during that interval?
  2. Which critical services share the same material provider, and how do we restore the business service when that provider is unavailable?
  3. After patching an exploited internet-facing application, how do we know an attacker’s relay and persistence have also been removed?
