What happened

Microsoft says a high-volume campaign on 13 November used messages disguised as Microsoft 365 service-health notifications, claiming held emails would be deleted unless recipients acted. It assessed the activity as likely credential phishing but did not confirm credential capture or post-compromise activity.

Why it matters

An outage gives an attacker a credible pretext to send urgent instructions just when staff expect unusual messages. Microsoft’s observations do not show that any particular firm was compromised. The decision for a firm is whether its people can verify a recovery request through a channel that does not depend on trusting the message itself.

What to do

Ask the incident communications owner to run one simulated provider-outage notice. Measure how many recipients use the published verification channel before acting, how long confirmation takes, and what happens when that channel is unavailable. Use the result to decide which recovery instructions need a clearer route.
