## Eight implementation implications

1. **Build every strategic page around a fixed evidence ladder:** one-sentence proposition → three decision themes → proof or method → risks/limitations → named next action. Do not introduce a second competing hero message.
2. **Use an editorial metadata contract for all insights:** topic, format, publication/update date, author, estimated reading time and evidence status. Render the same fields on cards, article headers and search results.
3. **Create a small, URL-addressable taxonomy:** no more than 5–7 primary themes and 4 content formats initially. Filters must expose result counts, preserve state in the URL and work by keyboard without JavaScript-only semantics.
4. **Place trust beside the claim:** show source, “as of” date, sample/method note and relevant caveat immediately after statistics or charts; reserve the footer for entity-wide legal text.
5. **Use two density modes:** spacious narrative sections for proposition and interpretation; compact, aligned tables/lists for evidence. On mobile, preserve labels and units rather than converting every row into an unstructured card.
6. **Adopt a restrained motion budget:** 90–120 ms hover/focus feedback, 150–240 ms disclosure transitions, no essential information revealed only by animation, and a static equivalent under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Avoid autoplay and scroll-jacking.
7. **Make progressive disclosure accessible by default:** native headings/landmarks, a visible skip link, persistent focus indicators, labelled expand/collapse controls, announced result counts, sortable-table semantics and text alternatives for diagrams.
8. **Separate evidence types in navigation and presentation:** “Insights” (analysis), “Client outcomes” (case evidence), “Methods” (how work is done) and “Services” (commercial offer) should be distinct but cross-linked, preventing marketing claims from masquerading as research.

## Access and interpretation notes

- Conclusions are based on public, first-party pages and their publicly rendered states on the review date. No roundup or imitation sites were used.
- Bloomberg's root-domain search request returned a non-retryable `robots.txt` block; its direct first-party commodities page was accessible and is the only Bloomberg state assessed.
- Material's two cited first-party URLs returned HTTP 200, but the text extractor exposed minimal page content; the matrix therefore uses only their system-level role and does not infer undocumented component details.
- Live homepages and feeds change. Structural patterns are durable; current headlines, statistics and content ordering should not be treated as permanent specifications.
