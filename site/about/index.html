Method

Source discipline

Official sources are preferred. Secondary reporting is labelled as monitoring signal. Weekly judgement is archived so the reasoning remains visible.

Source method

What makes the brief different from a feed

The credibility comes from explicit judgement: primary sources are preferred, secondary reporting is labelled rather than treated as settled fact, every important item is translated toward an owner or evidence prompt, and each week's issue is archived so the reasoning stays checkable.

Primary

Regulators, central banks, standard setters, official company or research sources

Used for rules, deadlines, enforcement, policy movement, and official claims.

Specialist

Legal, risk, technology, cyber, payments, and market implementation sources

Used for context, practical read-across, and implementation detail.

Press

General media and market reporting

Used for incidents, market colour, and early weak signals, with care around claims and wording.

Coverage and cadence

How to read what is here — and what is not

The site is selective by design. It shows the signals that cleared the editorial threshold, keeps useful memory visible, and makes gaps in coverage part of the interpretation rather than hiding them behind a confident blank page.

Eight watch themes

A shared map

AI, resilience, third-party risk, markets, financial crime, cyber, technology failure, and data are reviewed as connected operating risks.

Sunday refresh

Ready for Monday

The Top 5 is refreshed and checked on Sunday, with dates and source trails visible so the Monday LinkedIn post can point to a finished edition.

Curated memory

Still material, not stale

Some signals remain because they still change a live decision, control, or committee question. They are reviewed again rather than left to drift indefinitely.

Quiet this run

Not proof of no activity

A quiet theme means no reviewed material signal was published from that edition. It does not mean every relevant source was silent or every risk disappeared.

When source coverage is incomplete or classifications do not meet the publication standard, the safe answer is to show the limitation and use the last reviewed archive while the evidence is corrected.

About the author

St Georges Strategy is written by Ben St Georges, who has spent over two decades leading operational risk, compliance, audit, and governance across financial institutions in Paris, Hong Kong, Tokyo, Montreal, and London.

The throughline of that work, and of this site, is moving risk management from control to cognition: applying data, AI, and human judgement to anticipate issues before they occur. Written in a personal capacity; views are the author's own.
