Live edition · 1 Nov 2026 · Vol. XX

Policy adoption is not control evidence.

The Bank of England and FCA’s new survey finds AI policies are more common than several post-deployment controls. The management question is whether monitoring, leakage testing and review continue to change decisions after an AI system goes live.

Five-minute read / one-minute scan

The weekly readout

Do production agents have post-deployment control evidence?

The survey is aggregate and descriptive. It does not show whether firms with deployed agents are the same firms reporting monitoring, leakage testing or review, or whether those controls work.

01 / What happened

AI policies are more widely reported than post-deployment checks.

In the Bank of England and FCA’s 2026 survey, 94% of full-survey respondents reported firm-wide AI policies. Ongoing monitoring, leakage testing, and periodic review or recertification were each reported by about half. On agent adoption, 80% reported agents currently deployed. In the separate autonomy question, 80% reported decision-support agents in production and 2% broad autonomy. Percentages use valid question-level responses.

02 / Why it matters

Agent deployment and control coverage are not linked firm by firm.

The report gives aggregate, question-level results; it does not show whether firms reporting production agents are the same firms reporting monitoring, leakage testing or review. That gap matters even for decision-support agents: their recommendations can shape a workflow before the human makes and executes the decision. A firm-wide policy count cannot show whether those production workflows receive the controls after go-live.

03 / What changed outside the survey

A compromised AI gateway package could run at Python startup.

Microsoft’s analysis says LiteLLM 1.107.0 added malicious startup files that could execute even without an application importing LiteLLM. Exposure may include developer and build environments, not only active gateways.

04 / What to do

Put the agent denominator beside the control evidence.

Ask the AI governance owner for counts and denominators: how many production agents are monitored, leakage-tested and reviewed or recertified? Sample one material decision-support agent; trace its latest alert—or a controlled leakage test—to the human decision, time to review and any change to the recommendation or workflow. Set an owner and next-review date for each uncovered material agent.

  • Count production agents in scope and identify the operating owner.
  • Report how many have ongoing monitoring, leakage testing and periodic review or recertification, with a denominator for each.
  • Sample one material decision-support agent and trace an alert or controlled test to the human decision and resulting change.
  • Assign an owner and next-review date to each uncovered material agent.

Question 01 / Production-agent coverage

How many production agents have monitoring, leakage testing and periodic review—and what happened when the last exception surfaced?

Request counts and denominators for each control, then trace one decision-support agent’s alert or controlled test through to the human decision and resulting change.

Question 02 / Vendor change control

How do you validate and reverse a high-materiality vendor model update before it reaches a critical workflow?

Show the test, the rollback trigger and evidence that the rollback path works. The survey reports variation; it does not establish that every firm’s process is effective.

Question 03 / Software supply chain

If a Python environment ran LiteLLM 1.107.0, can you identify the credentials it could reach without starting Python there?

Microsoft advises investigators not to run Python or pip inside a suspect environment. Preserve evidence, identify reachable secrets and contain affected identities.

Reading the signals

Three different claims, three different tests

Survey responses, provider disclosures and incident research answer different questions. Keep their evidence boundaries visible.

Supervisory evidence

Reported policy coverage is not operating assurance.

The Bank of England/FCA results identify where respondents report governance controls. Boards still need evidence from production workflows about coverage, exceptions and resulting changes.

Source / 2026 AI survey

Software supply chain

Package risk may reach environments beyond the application.

Microsoft’s analysis says Python startup hooks in the compromised release could execute without an application importing LiteLLM. Remediation should account for affected runtimes, startup files and accessible credentials.

Source / Microsoft Security Research

Provider disclosure

Internal research containment does not establish customer deployment assurance.

OpenAI describes high-compute red-teaming and layered controls in its own training environments. Treat that as a provider account and ask what independent scope and evidence apply to any service your firm uses.

Source / OpenAI

Executive challenge

Three questions from the week

Use one real production example to see whether post-deployment control changes are visible and actionable.

  1. Which monitoring event changed a decision in a material AI workflow, and what changed afterward?
  2. What happens when a vendor update fails regression testing or a rollback trigger is met?
  3. Can incident response determine which environments and credentials were exposed by a compromised AI software dependency?
