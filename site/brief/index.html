Weekly brief / 27 Sept 2026

The dependency is now the decision.

Five public developments that make a simple point: an external model, provider, market utility or component can now sit inside the decision path — and inside the control boundary.

Five-minute read / one-minute scan

The weekly readout

The dependency chain is the control.

The useful leadership question is not whether a supplier or model was approved. It is whether the dependencies that make a live outcome possible can be seen, challenged and paused.

01 / What happened

External services are moving into the decision path.

Anthropic has published a low-stakes book-trading experiment about agents acting on users’ behalf; ASIC has strengthened AI trading safeguards; the Bank of England has consulted on supervisory fees for existing payment-system oversight; ENISA has examined dependencies; and CISA has added newly exploited vulnerabilities.

02 / Why it matters

A contract does not show the live point of failure.

Permission, market safeguards, service-provider supervision and an exploitable component can all alter a customer or trading outcome. The live chain matters more than the label on the contract.

03 / What to do

Trace one journey from outcome to dependency.

Choose one customer or trading outcome. Follow it through every external model, data feed, provider and component that can change it.

04 / Evidence

Test the power to pause it.

Put the chain beside the escalation call tree. The useful evidence is who can see the exposure, intervene and decide before the external weakness becomes an incident.

Reading the signals

Where the dependency sits

Three short read-across points for the next leadership conversation.

Delegated action

A low-stakes agent experiment still surfaces useful control questions.

Project Swap is not a financial-market deployment. Its value here is narrower: permissions, intervention and action records should be tested before an agent is trusted with a consequential decision.

Source / Anthropic, 24 Sept

Operating perimeter

The fees consultation applies to an existing supervisory perimeter.

Recognised payment systems and specified service providers are already within the Bank’s supervisory remit. The current consultation concerns the 2026/27 fees, not a new extension of that perimeter.

Source / Bank of England, 22 Sept

Containment

Dependency concentration and exploited components expose the same weakness.

A service-chain failure and a newly exploited flaw are manageable only when the affected dependencies can be identified, isolated and escalated in time.

Source / ENISA, 22 SeptSource / CISA, 25 Sept

Executive challenge

Three questions from the week

Carry one into a committee, 1:1 or control review.

  1. Which external dependency can change one decision-critical outcome — and who can halt it?
  2. Where can automation influence a trading or customer outcome without a tested override and a reconstructable record of why it acted?
  3. When an exploit or supplier problem appears, can we identify every affected dependency and decide on containment within one business day?
